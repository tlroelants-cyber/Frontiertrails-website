The High Mountains of Spain: Pyrenees, Picos de Europa & Sierra Nevada by Car

Where Spain climbs past 3,000 metres.

Sign up for your dream trip

Spain is not all beaches and plains. Along its northern edge, the Pyrenees rise into a wall of granite and limestone with peaks above 3,000 metres, hundreds of glacial lakes and valleys that only open up once the snow melts. Further west, the Picos de Europa stack sharp limestone towers just 20 kilometres from the Atlantic. And in the far south, the Sierra Nevada holds the highest summit of mainland Spain above Granada.

This is the landscape for people who want to earn their views. Drive up winding passes in the morning, hike to a lake that is still half frozen in June, and be back in a stone village for dinner. The best of it is spread over remote valleys with few buses and long distances between trailheads.

From Barcelona, the Catalan Pyrenees start less than two hours away. Aragon's Ordesa and the French side are a natural extension, and the Picos fit perfectly into a two-week loop across the north.

Best season Late June – October

Highlights

  1. 01

    Aigüestortes i Estany de Sant Maurici National Park

    Catalan Pyrenees (Lleida) · Best: Jun–Oct

    Catalonia's only national park: more than 200 lakes, granite peaks and meadows full of streams. The Sant Maurici lake under the twin Encantats peaks is the classic image.

    • hiking
    • lakes
  2. 02

    Vall Fosca & Estany Gento

    Pallars Jussà (Lleida) · Best: Jul–Sep

    A quiet valley with a cable car up to a chain of high lakes and a flat trail along an old railway built for a dam project. One of the easiest ways into the high lake country.

    • hiking
    • lakes
  3. 03

    Pedraforca

    Berguedà (Barcelona) · Best: May–Oct

    A forked limestone giant that looks unclimbable from below. Hike around its base or scramble to the top for views across the Cadí range.

    • hiking
    • views
  4. 04

    Vall de Núria

    Ripollès (Girona) · Best: Jun–Oct

    A high valley reached by rack railway, with trails up to Puigmal on the French border.

    • hiking
    • families
  5. 05

    Pica d'Estats

    Vall Ferrera (Lleida) · Best: Jul–Sep

    The highest peak in Catalonia (3,143 m) on the border with France. A long, demanding day through lakes and boulder fields to a summit with a view over half the Pyrenees.

    • summit hike
  6. 06

    Congost de Mont-rebei

    Montsec (Lleida / Huesca) · Best: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov

    A gorge with walls up to 500 metres, a path carved into the rock face and hanging bridges. The Aragonese side adds the vertiginous wooden stairs of Montfalcó.

    • hiking
    • views
  7. 07

    Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park

    Sobrarbe (Huesca) · Best: Jun–Oct

    A glacial canyon lined with beech forest and waterfalls under the Monte Perdido massif, part of a UNESCO World Heritage site shared with France. The walk to the Cola de Caballo falls is one of Spain's great day hikes.

    • hiking
    • waterfalls
  8. 08

    Aigualluts & the Aneto massif

    Benasque valley (Huesca) · Best: Jul–Sep

    A meadow where the river disappears into a sinkhole, at the foot of Aneto, the highest peak of the Pyrenees.

    • hiking
    • waterfalls
  9. 09

    Mallos de Riglos

    Hoya de Huesca · Best: Oct–May

    Red conglomerate towers up to 300 metres high rising straight out of the village. Vultures circle the walls; climbers hang off them.

    • views
    • climbing
    • short hikes
  10. 10

    Cirque de Gavarnie & Brèche de Roland (France)

    Hautes-Pyrénées · Best: Jun–Oct

    A vast amphitheatre of cliffs with one of Europe's tallest waterfalls. Above it, the Brèche de Roland: a 100-metre gap in the ridge on the Spain–France border.

    • hiking
    • views
  11. 11

    Ruta del Cares

    Picos de Europa (Asturias / León) · Best: May–Oct

    About 12 km (one way) of path cut into the side of a limestone gorge between Poncebos and Caín, with tunnels, bridges and a drop to the river far below.

    • hiking
  12. 12

    Sierra Nevada & the Alpujarras

    Granada · Best: Jun–Oct (hiking)

    Mainland Spain's highest peaks above the white villages of the Alpujarras. In summer a guided park minibus takes hikers high up the mountain to start the Mulhacén ascent.

    • villages

What to do

Hiking & lakes

Day hikes to glacial lakes in Aigüestortes, Vall Fosca and the Carlit lakes just over the French border; canyon walks in Ordesa and the Cares.

Summits

Pica d'Estats, Puigmal, Pedraforca, Monte Perdido (experienced hikers), Mulhacén.

Via ferrata

Sorrosal waterfall in Broto (beginner-friendly), Foradada del Toscar in Ribagorza and many routes around Montsec.

Gorges & catwalks

Mont-rebei and the Montfalcó stairs; the Gorges de la Carança in France.

Scenic drives

Mountain passes such as the Bonaigua (Val d'Aran) and the Port de Cabús track from Andorra into Catalonia's Tor valley (4x4 on the Spanish side).

Wildlife

Vultures at Riglos and Mont-rebei; marmots and chamois above the tree line; the red deer rut in the Pre-Pyrenees (Boumort) in September–October.

  • Late June – September: high trails snow-free, lakes at their best, busiest in August.

On the road

  • Mountain roads are slow. Plan on 40–50 km/h average once you leave the highway.
  • Check access before you go
    • Pica d'Estats (Vall Ferrera): the car parks at the head of the valley work with paid reservations in high season.
    • Picos de Europa – Lagos de Covadonga: on high-season dates the road is closed to private cars all day and you take the bus from Cangas de Onís.
    • Sierra Nevada: the old high road between Hoya del Portillo and Hoya de la Mora is closed to traffic; in summer a guided park minibus runs from Capileira (booking needed).
    • Lac des Bouillouses (France): road closed to cars during the day in July–August; shuttle from Pla de Barrès.
  • Dirt tracks in Catalonia, driving is only allowed on open roads and authorised tracks; narrow tracks and off-road driving are prohibited. Many valley-head tracks have barriers.
  • Fuel & signal fill up in the main valley towns (Sort, Vielha, Aínsa, Benasque). Phone coverage disappears in many upper valleys; download offline maps.
  • Weather afternoon thunderstorms are common in July–August. Start early.

FAQ

When is the best time to visit the Spanish Pyrenees?

From late June to early October, when the high trails are free of snow. September is ideal: stable weather, fewer visitors and the first autumn colours.

Do I need a 4x4 in the Pyrenees?

Not for the main valleys and trailheads, which are paved. A higher, capable car makes the gravel access roads and forest tracks easier and opens up more remote starting points.

Can you drive to the Lagos de Covadonga in the Picos de Europa?

Only outside the regulated dates. In high season the road is closed to private cars and a shuttle bus runs from Cangas de Onís. Check the official calendar before you go.

How many days do I need for the Pyrenees?

Three days is enough for a taste of the Catalan side. Five to seven days lets you combine Catalonia, Aragon (Ordesa, Benasque) and the French Gavarnie.

What is the highest mountain in the Pyrenees?

Aneto (3,404 m) in the Benasque valley, Aragon. The highest peak in Catalonia is the Pica d'Estats (3,143 m).
