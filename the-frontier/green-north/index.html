The Green North: A Road Trip Through Atlantic Spain

Atlantic Spain: rain, beech forests and wild cliffs.

Sign up for your dream trip

Cross the mountains from the dry heart of Spain and everything turns green. The Atlantic north gets the rain the rest of the country doesn't, and it shows: dense beech forests, emerald valleys full of grazing cows, fishing villages stacked against the hills and a coastline that the ocean has carved into arches, caves and islands.

From the Basque Country to Galicia, this is a road trip of contrasts packed into a narrow strip. In one day you can walk through one of Europe's largest beech-fir forests, drive to a hermitage on a rock in the sea and end with seafood in a harbour town. Further west, Galicia feels like the edge of the world – the Costa da Morte was long thought to be exactly that.

It is the furthest landscape from Barcelona, which is why it works best as the backbone of a two-week trip: cross the Pyrenees, follow the coast west and come back through the interior.

Best season May – September

Highlights

  1. 01

    Selva de Irati

    Navarra Pyrenees · Best: May–Jun, Oct

    One of the largest beech-fir forests in Europe, with trails to the Irabia reservoir. In October the whole forest turns copper and gold.

    • autumn colour
  2. 02

    Baztan valley & Elizondo

    Navarra · Best: May–Oct

    Green hills, stone farmhouses and the river town of Elizondo, gateway to the rocky ridges of Monte Iparla on the French border.

    • villages
    • hiking
  3. 03

    San Juan de Gaztelugatxe

    Bizkaia coast · Best: spring & autumn

    A tiny hermitage on a rocky islet, reached by a stone bridge and 241 steps. Known worldwide as Dragonstone from Game of Thrones.

    • short walk
  4. 04

    Salto del Nervión

    Burgos / Álava border · Best: winter & spring (after rain)

    A cliff-edge viewpoint over one of Spain's highest waterfalls, at its most powerful after rain.

    • views
  5. 05

    Orbaneja del Castillo

    Burgos · Best: Apr–Oct

    A village wedged into a limestone canyon, with a waterfall running right through the middle of it.

    • waterfall
  6. 06

    Ojo Guareña

    Burgos · Best: all year

    One of the longest cave systems in Spain, with a hermitage built into the rock at its entrance.

    • caves
  7. 07

    Cudillero

    Asturias coast · Best: May–Sep

    A fishing village of coloured houses climbing up a steep amphitheatre around the harbour.

    • village
    • coast
  8. 08

    Cabo Busto & the Asturian cliffs

    Asturias coast · Best: May–Sep

    A lighthouse on high green cliffs, on a stretch of coast with coves and sea stacks.

    • coastal walks
    • views
  9. 09

    As Catedrais beach

    Ribadeo, Lugo · Best: low tide, spring–autumn

    Natural rock arches up to 30 metres high that look like cathedral buttresses. Walk the beach at low tide, see it from the cliffs at high tide.

    • views
  10. 10

    Ribeira Sacra & the Sil Canyon

    Lugo / Ourense · Best: May–Oct

    Terraced vineyards on impossibly steep canyon walls, Romanesque monasteries and viewpoints high above the river.

    • boat trip
    • wine
  11. 11

    Las Médulas

    El Bierzo, León · Best: Apr–Jun, Sep–Nov

    Red peaks and tunnels left by the largest gold mine of the Roman Empire, now a UNESCO World Heritage site.

    • views
  12. 12

    Costa da Morte

    A Coruña · Best: May–Sep

    The "Coast of Death", named for its shipwrecks: granite capes, lighthouses and huge empty beaches facing the open Atlantic, ending at Fisterra.

What to do

Forest walks

Irati in autumn, the beech woods of Baztan.

Coastal hikes

Clifftop trails around Cabo Busto, Gaztelugatxe and the Costa da Morte.

Beaches & tides

As Catedrais at low tide; wild Atlantic beaches in Galicia (cold water, big waves).

Surfing

The Basque and Cantabrian coasts have consistent Atlantic swell.

Caves & canyons

Ojo Guareña, the Orbaneja canyon, the Sil gorge by boat.

Food stops

Pintxos in the Basque Country, cider houses in Asturias, seafood and Ribeira Sacra wine in Galicia.

On the road

  • Distances are long. Barcelona to Galicia is a full day's drive. Build the north into a loop rather than an out-and-back.
  • Check access before you go
    • As Catedrais: free reservation needed during Holy Week and from 1 July to 30 September.
    • San Juan de Gaztelugatxe: free online ticket required on controlled-access days (high season); other days are open access.
    • Selva de Irati: the main access car parks charge a fee in high season.
    • Lagos de Covadonga (Picos): closed to private cars on high-season dates; take the bus from Cangas de Onís.
  • Roads coastal roads are narrow and winding; allow extra time between villages.
  • Tides check tide tables for As Catedrais and other beaches; some are only walkable at low tide.

FAQ

When is the best time for a road trip in Northern Spain?

May to September for the best weather and long days. October is quieter and brings autumn colour to the forests, but expect more rain.

How many days do you need for Northern Spain?

At least a week to see the Basque coast, Picos de Europa and Asturias. Ten to fourteen days lets you continue into Galicia and loop back through the interior.

Do you need a reservation for As Catedrais beach?

Yes, during Holy Week and from 1 July to 30 September. The reservation is free. Outside those periods you can visit without one.

Do you need a ticket for San Juan de Gaztelugatxe?

On controlled-access days in high season you need a free online ticket with a time slot. On other days access is free without booking.

Does it rain a lot in Northern Spain?

Yes, more than anywhere else in Spain, which is why it's so green. Summer is the driest period, but bring rain gear year-round.
