The Mediterranean: Sierras, River Gorges & Hidden Coves from Catalonia to Andalusia

Sun-baked sierras, river gorges and hidden coves.

Sign up for your dream trip

Behind Spain's famous coastline lies a second Mediterranean that most visitors never see. Limestone sierras rise straight out of the sea, rivers have cut swimmable gorges into the mountains, and villages cling to cliffs, volcanoes and canyon edges. It's all just a short drive from the beach towns.

Within two hours of Barcelona you can stand on the easternmost point of mainland Spain at Cap de Creus, walk into an extinct volcano in the Garrotxa or climb a via ferrata on Montserrat. Head south and the Ebro Delta, the gorges of Els Ports and Valencia's thermal rivers follow, all the way to the white villages, canyons and mountain parks of Andalusia.

The Mediterranean is the landscape you can do almost any time of year: spring and autumn are perfect, and even in winter the sun is out.

Best season All year (best March–June and September–November)

Highlights

  1. 01

    Cap de Creus & Cadaqués

    Costa Brava (Girona) · Best: Apr–Jun, Sep–Oct

    The easternmost point of mainland Spain: wind-sculpted rock, hidden coves and the whitewashed village of Cadaqués.

    • swimming
  2. 02

    Garrotxa Volcanic Zone

    Olot (Girona) · Best: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov

    Dozens of extinct volcanic cones under beech forest. Walk into the Santa Margarida crater, with a small chapel in the middle, or up the Croscat volcano.

    • hiking
    • forest
  3. 03

    Sant Aniol d'Aguja & Alta Garrotxa

    Girona · Best: May–Oct

    A wild river valley with turquoise pools, Romanesque bridges and a hermitage deep in the mountains.

    • hiking
    • river swimming
  4. 04

    Cingles de Tavertet

    Osona (Barcelona) · Best: all year

    A stone village on the edge of sheer cliffs above the Sau reservoir, with waterfalls and viewpoints along the rim.

    • views
    • short hikes
  5. 05

    Montserrat

    Barcelona · Best: all year

    A saw-toothed massif of conglomerate pinnacles with a monastery halfway up. Hike the ridges or try the Canal de les Dames via ferrata.

    • via ferrata
    • hiking
    • climbing
  6. 06

    Siurana & the Priorat

    Tarragona · Best: Oct–May

    A tiny village on a cliff above a turquoise reservoir, at the heart of one of the world's top sport-climbing regions. Wine country all around.

    • views
    • climbing
    • villages
  7. 07

    Els Ports & the Estrets d'Arnes

    Tarragona / Teruel · Best: May–Oct

    Rugged limestone mountains with a narrow rock-walled gorge and the Toll Blau pool. Nearby, the warm natural pool of Fontcalda.

    • gorge hike
    • swimming
  8. 08

    Ebro Delta

    Tarragona · Best: Apr–Oct

    Flat rice fields, lagoons full of flamingos and wild sandbar beaches where the Ebro meets the sea.

    • wildlife
    • cycling
    • beaches
  9. 09

    Montanejos & the Fuente de los Baños

    Castellón · Best: May–Oct

    A river pool with water at a steady mild temperature year-round, in a gorge with climbing walls and trails.

    • river swimming
    • hiking
  10. 10

    Font dels Xorros & the Serra de Bernia

    Alicante · Best: Oct–May

    A spring tumbling out of the rock below the Bernia ridge, with a loop past an old fort and views down to the Costa Blanca.

    • hiking
    • views
  11. 11

    Ronda & Setenil de las Bodegas

    Málaga / Cádiz · Best: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov

    Ronda's bridge over a 100-metre gorge, and Setenil, where houses are built into the overhang of a river cliff.

    • villages
    • views
  12. 12

    Caminito del Rey

    Málaga · Best: Oct–May

    A walkway bolted to the walls of the Gaitanes gorge, once called the most dangerous path in the world and now one of Spain's most popular walks. Book tickets well in advance.

    • hiking
    • gorge walk
  13. 13

    Sierras de Cazorla, Segura y Las Villas

    Jaén · Best: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov

    Spain's largest protected natural area: pine-covered mountains, the source of the Guadalquivir and some of the best wildlife watching in the south.

    • wildlife

What to do

Coastal hikes & coves

Cap de Creus and the Costa Brava coast paths.

Wild swimming

Sant Aniol, Toll Blau, Fontcalda, Montanejos.

Via ferrata

Montserrat, the Baumes Corcades near Centelles, the coastal Cala del Molí.

Climbing

Siurana, Margalef and Montserrat in Catalonia; El Chorro near the Caminito.

Volcanoes & forests

The craters and beech woods of the Garrotxa.

Gorge walks

Estrets d'Arnes, Caminito del Rey, Ronda's gorge.

Wildlife

Flamingos in the Ebro Delta; ibex and vultures in Els Ports and Cazorla.

Villages & food

Priorat wine, Cadaqués seafood, the white villages of Andalusia.

  • March – June & September – November: ideal for hiking, gorge walks and climbing.
  • July – August: great for swimming spots and the coast, but hot inland (Andalusia can exceed 40 °C) and busy at popular pools. Start early.
  • Winter: mild and sunny on the coast and in the south, which makes this the best landscape for a winter trip.

On the road

  • Easy driving, tricky parking. Most highlights are on paved roads, but popular spots (Cadaqués, Sant Aniol, Montanejos, Ronda) fill up fast on weekends and in summer. Arrive early.
  • Check access before you go
    • Caminito del Rey: tickets must be booked in advance (general entry around €10, guided around €18) and sell out weeks ahead.
    • Estrets d'Arnes: swimming is only allowed at the Toll Blau pool.
  • Dirt tracks in Catalonia, driving is only allowed on open roads and authorised tracks; narrow tracks and off-road driving are prohibited.
  • Fire season in summer, some forest areas and tracks close during high fire risk. Check local alerts before heading into the mountains.

FAQ

What are the best hikes near Barcelona?

Montserrat, the Garrotxa volcanoes, the Tavertet cliffs and the Cap de Creus coast are all within two hours. Sant Aniol d'Aguja adds a river hike with swimming pools.

Where can you go wild swimming in Spain?

Great spots include Sant Aniol d'Aguja and the Alta Garrotxa in Girona, the Toll Blau pool in Els Ports, the warm pool of Fontcalda and the Fuente de los Baños in Montanejos.

Do you need tickets for the Caminito del Rey?

Yes. Tickets must be booked in advance on the official website and often sell out weeks ahead, especially in spring and autumn.

When is the best time for a road trip along Mediterranean Spain?

Spring (March–June) and autumn (September–November) are best. Summer is hot inland and busy on the coast; winter is mild and sunny, especially in the south.

Is the Costa Brava good for hiking?

Yes. The coastal paths around Cap de Creus and Cadaqués combine hiking with hidden coves for swimming, and the volcanic Garrotxa is just inland.
