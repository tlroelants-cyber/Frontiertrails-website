The Dry Interior: Spain's Deserts, Badlands & Red Canyons

Ghost villages, red canyons, no one around.

Sign up for your dream trip

Somewhere between the Pyrenees and the Mediterranean, Spain empties out. The high plateau of the interior is one of the least populated areas in Europe: long straight roads, abandoned villages on the hilltops and almost no traffic. Push further and the land dries into something you don't expect in Europe at all – clay badlands, white desert plains, red canyons and volcanic coast.

The Dry Interior brings together two sides of the same Spain. In the north, the Bardenas Reales and Monegros deserts lie in the Ebro valley, a few hours from Barcelona. In the middle, Teruel and Cuenca hide medieval towns, turquoise lakes and rust-red gorges. And in the south-east, Granada, Almería and Murcia hold some of the driest landscapes in Europe, from the badlands of Gorafe to the spaghetti-western sets of Tabernas.

This is road-trip country in its purest form: the drive is the point. Low sun, empty horizons and the feeling of having a whole landscape to yourself.

Best season October – May (avoid July–August)

Highlights

  1. 01

    Bardenas Reales

    Navarra · Best: Oct–May

    A semi-desert of eroded clay towers and plateaus, protected as a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. A signposted loop of about 55 km leads past its icon, the clay chimney of Castildetierra.

    Monegros

    Huesca / Zaragoza · Best: Oct–Apr

    Steppe and gypsum hills that feel more North African than European, crossed by long, empty roads and farm tracks.

    • solitude
  3. 03

    Sierra de Armantes

    Calatayud (Zaragoza) · Best: Oct–May

    Red and ochre badlands carved into towers and ravines, just outside Calatayud.

    • photography

    Albarracín

    Teruel · Best: Apr–Jun, Sep–Nov

    A pink medieval town on a rock above a river gorge, surrounded by pine forest full of red sandstone boulders.

  5. 05

    Cañón Rojo (Rambla Barrachina)

    near Teruel · Best: Oct–May

    A narrow canyon of blood-red earth walls just outside Teruel, one of the most surreal walks in the interior.

  6. 06

    Cañada del Hoyo lakes

    Serranía de Cuenca · Best: Apr–Jun, Sep–Oct

    Round karst lakes sunk into the forest, each with its own shade of blue, green or white.

    • views
  7. 07

    Nacimiento del Río Cuervo

    Serranía de Cuenca · Best: spring (after snowmelt)

    The source of a river spilling over mossy tufa ledges in a cascade of small waterfalls.

  8. 08

    Las Cárcavas

    Guadalajara · Best: Oct–May

    Dark, deeply folded badlands of ridges and gullies, especially striking at sunset.

    • photography
  9. 09

    Barrancos de Gebas

    Murcia · Best: Nov–Apr

    A "lunar" landscape of pale marl ridges and gullies at the foot of the Sierra Espuña.

  10. 10

    Desierto de Gorafe

    Granada · Best: Oct–May

    Red and white badlands cut by canyons, scattered with hundreds of prehistoric dolmens.

  11. 11

    Tabernas Desert

    Almería · Best: Oct–Apr

    Dry riverbeds and bare hills where dozens of spaghetti westerns were filmed. Some of the old film sets still stand.

    Cabo de Gata

    Almería · Best: Mar–Jun, Sep–Nov

    A volcanic coastline where the desert meets the sea: dunes, black rock, salt flats with flamingos and some of the wildest beaches on the Mediterranean.

What to do

Desert driving

Bardenas loop, Monegros roads, Tabernas and Gorafe backroads.

Canyon hikes

Cañón Rojo, Sierra de Armantes, the gorges around Albarracín.

Photography

Low winter light on badlands; sunsets at Las Cárcavas and Gorafe.

Stargazing

The emptiest parts of the interior and Almería have some of the darkest skies in Spain.

Bouldering & climbing

The sandstone boulders of Albarracín are internationally known.

Swimming & snorkelling

Clear water and volcanic coves at Cabo de Gata.

History

Medieval Albarracín, Gorafe's dolmens, the film sets of Tabernas.

  • October – May: the season for the drylands. Mild days, low light, empty roads.
  • Spring: green tints on the badlands and full rivers in Cuenca.
  • Summer: temperatures regularly pass 40 °C in Monegros, Bardenas and Almería. Avoid July–August, or stick to early mornings.
  • Winter: sunny but cold at night on the high plateau (Teruel and Cuenca can drop below zero).

On the road

  • Empty means empty. Fuel stations can be 50+ km apart. Fill up whenever you can and carry water.
  • Check access before you go
    • Bardenas Reales: open from 8:00 until one hour before sunset. Vehicles must stay on the marked routes, with speed limits on the tracks. Parts of the park close to visitors during the bird-nesting season, roughly February to August.
    • Cabo de Gata (Genoveses & Mónsul beaches): car access is restricted in summer; a shuttle bus runs from San José.
  • Tracks many highlights are reached by gravel roads. Drive slowly, avoid tracks after heavy rain (clay turns to mud), and respect "no entry" signs; much of this land is private farmland.
  • Heat no shade in the badlands. Bring a hat, sunscreen and more water than you think.

FAQ

Is there a desert in Spain?

Yes. Spain has several semi-arid deserts, including the Bardenas Reales in Navarra, Monegros in Aragon, the Tabernas Desert in Almería and the badlands of Gorafe in Granada.

When is the best time to visit Spain's deserts?

From October to May. Summer temperatures often exceed 40 °C, which makes hiking and exploring uncomfortable and unsafe.

Can you drive through the Bardenas Reales?

Yes, on the marked routes, including a signposted loop of about 55 km. The park is open from 8:00 until an hour before sunset; leaving the marked tracks is not allowed.

Do you need a 4x4 in the Bardenas Reales or Tabernas?

The main routes are gravel roads that a normal car can manage in dry weather. A higher, capable car is more comfortable and handles rougher tracks and post-rain conditions better.

What is there to see in Teruel?

The medieval town of Albarracín, the red Cañón Rojo canyon, the Matarraña region with El Parrizal gorge, and endless empty roads across the high plateau.
